# Industrial Conveyor & Material Handling Manufacturers & Models

A comprehensive reference list of bulk-materials conveyor and material-handling OEMs — belt conveyors, belt manufacturers, bucket elevators, screw/drag/apron/pneumatic/vibratory conveyors, stacker-reclaimers, ship loaders, IPCC (in-pit crushing & conveying) systems, plus belt accessories (idlers, pulleys, cleaners, impact beds, splicing). Brand lineage matters intensely in this category because belt manufacturing has consolidated under Michelin, Continental, Yokohama, and Bridgestone umbrellas, while the bulk-handling systems side has seen BEUMER absorb FAM (2022), Sandvik pull in Schenck Process Mining (2022), Komatsu absorb Joy Global (2017), and Continental AG itself now preparing to spin off or sell ContiTech entirely in 2026 after already spinning off its automotive arm (Aumovio) in September 2025. On the light-duty side, warehouse / unit-handling conveyor has consolidated under Honeywell Intelligrated, Dematic (KION), Daifuku, Murata, SSI Schaefer, and others — a completely different OEM set from the bulk side.

## Tier 1 — Global Market Leaders (Bulk Handling Systems)

The four companies that dominate large-scale bulk-handling system engineering worldwide: BEUMER Group, TAKRAF (Tenova), thyssenkrupp Mining Technologies, and Metso. These are full-service system integrators that design, manufacture, and install complete conveyor-stockyard-loading complexes for mining, cement, ports, and power generation.

### BEUMER Group (Germany, Beckum)
Family-owned (since 1935), 100% self-financed; one of the most aggressive acquirers in bulk handling. In May/June 2022, BEUMER Group acquired the Magdeburg-based FAM Group — one of the world's leading full-range bulk handling and processing equipment suppliers — giving BEUMER full control of FAM's stacker-reclaimer, bucket-wheel excavator, IPCC, and ship loader portfolio. In June 2023, BEUMER acquired The Hendrik Group Inc. (US), specialists in air-supported belt conveyors. Also acquired Crisplant (2009) and Enexco Teknologies India (2011).

## Conveyor Capacity & Design Quick Reference

Conveyor belt capacity is determined by belt width, speed, trough angle, surcharge angle, and material density. Standard CEMA ranges for secondary-market context:

| Belt Width | Typical Capacity Range (at 400 fpm, 35° trough, 20° surcharge, 100 lb/ft³) |
|---|---|
| **18 in (457 mm)** | ~120 tph |
| **24 in (610 mm)** | ~235 tph |
| **30 in (762 mm)** | ~385 tph |
| **36 in (914 mm)** | ~570 tph |
| **42 in (1067 mm)** | ~790 tph |
| **48 in (1219 mm)** | ~1,050 tph |
| **54 in (1372 mm)** | ~1,335 tph |
| **60 in (1524 mm)** | ~1,670 tph |
| **72 in (1829 mm)** | ~2,430 tph |
| **84 in (2134 mm)** | ~3,330 tph |
| **96 in (2438 mm)** | ~4,380 tph |
| **120 in (3048 mm)** | ~6,940 tph (rare; reserved for iron ore mega-projects) |

Standard belt speeds: 2.5–5.0 m/s (500–1000 fpm) for most bulk; up to 7+ m/s for high-capacity overland conveyors; 0.5–1.5 m/s for sluggish/sticky materials or transfer-point-rich systems.

Standard trough angles: 20°, 35°, 45° (most aggregates/mining use 35°); deep troughs (45°) increase capacity ~10–15% over 35° for same width/speed.

Standard horsepower sizing: Length-related HP (for tension + flexure + idler friction) + Height-related HP (lift) + Material-acceleration HP — CEMA calculation is industry standard.
